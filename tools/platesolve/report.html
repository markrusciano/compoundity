When it was taken

Pointing at the zenith makes the field centre’s right ascension equal to the local sidereal time. That reads 22h 15m 28s, which at this longitude puts the shutter at 00:22 EDT on 6 September 2026 — 04:22 UT.

One honest caveat: sidereal time repeats every day, so the clock time is only exact if the date is right. Each day earlier shifts the answer about 3 minutes 56 seconds later — a photo from 30 August would have been taken near 00:50. The patch of sky, though, is fixed regardless of the date.

Two details corroborate a deep-night exposure. The Moon was 7.4° below the horizon and only 29% lit, which is why so many faint stars survived a downtown sky. And no planet was anywhere near the frame — the nearest, Saturn, sat 50° away.

Nothing about the camera was assumed. The focal length falls out of the geometry: fixing two identified stars fixes the angle they subtend, and only one focal length reproduces their pixel separation. That it landed within a few percent of a 26 mm-equivalent iPhone main camera is a check on the whole solution, not an input to it.

Mean residual across the nine anchors is 0.92 px, about 67 arcseconds. The one visible outlier is Deneb, and that is expected rather than worrying: it is the brightest star in the picture, its core is saturated flat across several pixels, and a saturated core centroids less precisely than a faint one. The residuals track brightness, not position in the frame, which is the signature of a sound model.

The stronger test came afterwards. Coordinates for 48 further named stars were projected through the solution without being fitted to; 47 of them landed on a measured source, most within three pixels. The one miss, Fawaris, sits half-cut by the left edge.

Method

Sources were extracted by local-maximum detection against a robust local background, using the 16th-to-50th percentile spread as the noise estimate so that dense star fields would not inflate their own threshold. Candidate pairs among the brightest sources were matched against every catalogue pair; each pairing implies a focal length analytically, which then implies a full camera rotation. Each hypothesis was scored on how many bright catalogue stars it placed on measured sources, and the winner was refined by least squares over rotation, focal length and distortion.

Every one of the top hypotheses converged on the same field, and the fitted distortion coefficients went to zero — the expected result for an iPhone JPEG, which is already corrected in camera.

Star counts put the faintest sources near magnitude 9–10, though the JPEG’s crushed tone curve makes photometry from it rough. M31 is inside the frame but is not convincingly present in the pixel data; the marker shows where it is, not a detection.
